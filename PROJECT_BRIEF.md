# Answerable — Project Brief

## Product statement

**Answerable** is a visual technology-ecology game for educators, school leaders, higher-education audiences, and policy workshops. Players introduce a proposed technology into a living school system, apply the five technoskeptical questions, and produce an evidence-based Answerability Charter.

The game teaches that technoskepticism is neither rejection nor surrender. It is disciplined deliberation that keeps technological change answerable to human and educational purposes.

## Canonical source frame

- Neil Postman's five propositions about technological change.
- The five technoskeptical questions adapted by Dan Krutka and Scott Metzger and circulated through the Civics of Technology curriculum.
- Micah Miner's pedagogical-friction overlay distinguishing productive friction from exclusionary friction.
- Public primer: <https://minerclass.github.io/technoskepticism-primer/>

This game is a teaching synthesis, not a primary source and not a validated assessment instrument.

## Core game loop

1. Select a proposed school technology and two purposes it should serve.
2. Use the **Price Lens** to name what may be surrendered.
3. Use the **Distribution Lens** to map benefits, burdens, and power.
4. Use the **Dependency Lens** to expose what the technology demands.
5. Use the **Ripple Lens** to inspect ecological changes over time.
6. Use the **Myth Lens** to imagine alternatives and reopen choices treated as inevitable.
7. Make a council decision and generate an Answerability Charter.

## Interaction model

Each round uses a different interaction rather than repeating a conventional quiz:

- Reveal and select hidden trade-offs.
- Activate a stakeholder heat map.
- Cut or govern dependency connections.
- Move a time slider through second-order effects.
- Toggle the technology out of the system and compare alternatives.

## Visual model

The central board is a code-native school ecology composed of accessible HTML nodes and SVG connection lines. Its visual state changes with the current lens and player actions. A generated editorial illustration is used only for the opening and transition atmosphere.

## Initial scenarios

1. AI writing assistant
2. Automated tutoring platform
3. Predictive attendance dashboard
4. Phone-management system

## Non-negotiable guardrails

- Do not frame technology as automatically good or bad.
- Do not present one universal adoption verdict.
- Do not collect, store, or transmit player responses.
- Do not treat outcomes as research findings or validated assessment scores.
- Do not publish student, staff, participant, or district data.
- Preserve keyboard operation, reduced motion, readable contrast, and a structured alternative to visual-only meaning.
